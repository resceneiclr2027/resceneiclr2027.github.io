RESCENE

Visually Mediated VLA Alignment
through Latent Scene Editing

If a vehicle is removed from a scene, can a driving model describe and act in that changed world without reading the edit instruction at response time?

Read the abstract ↓

Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models map visual observations and language requests to actions. For language-defined situations that are not directly observed, an appropriate action alone cannot distinguish visual grounding from an instruction-to-action shortcut.

RESCENE makes this distinction testable by constructing the specified scene in visual feature space. A latent editor masks affected camera-history evidence and predicts DINOv3 features from context, ego motion, and annotation-derived spatial hints. A separate response pass receives the constructed features, task, and navigation inputs, but not the edit condition or editor state. It generates language and then predicts route and trajectory; response gradients stop at the feature interface.

Factual reconstruction, frozen-reader semantic supervision, and an inverse feature cycle constrain construction without paired edited RGB. Joint learning preserves a direct observed-feature path for ordinary driving. On Bench2Drive220, RESCENE gains 6.41 driving-score points and 4.70 percentage points in route completion over factual replay, with a higher success rate. These results support a visually mediated formulation, while authored-world tests expose language and action trade-offs.

Interpretation

Limitations and scope

Visual mediation is an explicit information-flow design. These tests provide evidence for its utility and show where that evidence is incomplete.

Edited images are not predicted

Construction is in DINOv3 feature space. CARLA case RGB is re-rendered from authored edits; it does not validate a uniquely correct latent world.

Spatial hints are supplied

The edited path uses annotation-derived actor boxes and local-map hints for hypothetical training and offline evaluation. This is not language-only editing or an online deployment demonstration.

Action and language diverge

Direct conditioning leads on paired QA and joint success; RESCENE has slightly worse observed-path open-loop waypoint error. The methods also differ in edited-feature losses.

No broad safety claim

Some collision and invariance cases remain difficult. Instruction-proximity scores do not prove request completion or refusal safety; main result tables have no confidence intervals.
