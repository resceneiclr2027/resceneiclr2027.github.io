01 / Overview

Abstract

Vision-language-action driving models can respond to language-defined situations without representing the described change in their visual state. A plausible hypothetical action alone therefore does not establish visual grounding.

RESCENE studies alignment through condition-exclusive visual mediation. A latent scene editor masks affected camera-history evidence and predicts DINO features on edited camera-time-patch support. A separate response pass receives assembled features, task, and navigation inputs—but neither the edit condition nor the editor state—and generates language followed by route and trajectory.

Factual feature reconstruction, frozen semantic supervision, and an inverse feature cycle constrain the constructed representation without paired counterfactual images. Matched comparisons, an independent feature reader, and same-world language–action tests are designed to distinguish visual mediation from shortcuts.

05 / Interpretation

The boundary of the claim.

The architecture makes visual mediation testable; it does not, by itself, demonstrate visual grounding.

No unique edited image

Reconstruction, semantic supervision, and the inverse cycle constrain features, not a physically complete counterfactual RGB scene.

No language-only deployment claim

Edited scenes use annotation-derived spatial hints in training and offline evaluation. Ordinary driving uses the observed path.

No single-score proof

Feature meaning, same-world language and action, and retained observed-scene driving must be assessed together.
